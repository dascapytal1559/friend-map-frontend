# Settings

`data/settings.json` holds the few things you can set by editing a file. It is optional, and so is every key in it; anything left out uses its default:

```json
{ "trendMonths": 12, "closenessHalfLifeDays": 90, "catchUpHourWorth": 50, "callMinimumMinutes": 5, "messageReciprocity": 1, "groupSharedPart": 0.5, "tripFullHours": 8 }
```

- `trendMonths` (default 12, any whole number from 3 to 240): how many months back each person's trend looks. It sets the trend on the hover card and how far back the comet trails on Orbits start, both at once: 6 for the last half year, 36 for three years.
- `closenessHalfLifeDays` (default 90, any whole number from 7 to 3650): how fast a message's weight in the closeness score fades, which sets how far from the sun everyone sits on Orbits. A message this many days old counts half, twice as old a quarter: 30 for a map of the last month or two, 365 for one that remembers years.
- `catchUpHourWorth` (default 50, any whole number from 0 to 1000): how many direct messages an hour caught up counts as in the closeness score on its date, a catch-up's hours times this; an hour of answered calls (the phone's call history, Messenger, Instagram, WhatsApp, Telegram) counts the same. It then fades by the same half-life. 0 leaves catch-ups and calls out of the score. Below says why this default.
- `messageReciprocity` (default 1, any number from 0 to 1, fractions too): how much a month's direct messages with someone count by how evenly you both wrote (below). 1 counts each month as 2 × √(yours × theirs), so a one-way month counts nothing; 0 counts every message 1, as the score did before; 0.5 is half of each.
- `groupSharedPart` (default 0.5, any number from 0 to 1, fractions too): how much of a hangout, or a call with several people (one you logged, or a WhatsApp three-way call), each of them shares whole; the rest is split equally among them (below). Each of n people takes s + (1 − s) ÷ n of its time: at 0.5, 75% each for two, 63% for four, 52% for twenty-five; 0 splits it equally (1/n each), as the score did before; 1 gives everyone all of it. One person always takes all of it, and group chats keep their own rule.
- `tripFullHours` (default 8, any number from 1 to 8784, fractions too): how many hours of a trip (a hangout over several days, see "Hangouts" under "People" in the life archive's README) count whole; past them its time together counts as F × √(hours ÷ F), so each hour more adds less (below). 8784, a whole year of days, counts every trip whole, as trips did at first. A hangout on one date always counts every hour whole.
- `callMinimumMinutes` (default 5, any whole number from 0 to 120): how long a day's answered calls with someone (from the phone's call history, see "Calls" under "The message archive" in the life archive's README, and Messenger, Instagram, WhatsApp and Telegram, all together) must add up to, in minutes, to count at all; shorter count nothing. 0 counts every answered call, voicemails included.

Reload the page after changing it. A value that is wrong (or a file that isn't valid JSON) uses the default instead, with a warning on the main view saying what is wrong, and `npm run check-settings` reports it too.

## How closeness is scored

The closeness score (`src/demos/closeness.ts`) is the distance on Orbits:

```
closeness = Σ over every month of direct messages:          direct × ½^(age ÷ closenessHalfLifeDays)
          + Σ over every group message between you and them: weight × ½^(age ÷ closenessHalfLifeDays)
          + Σ over every catch-up you had with them:          worth  × ½^(age ÷ closenessHalfLifeDays)

direct = (1 − r) × (yours + theirs) + r × 2√(yours × theirs)    that month's direct messages each way,
                                                                  r = messageReciprocity (1)
weight = your share ÷ others   a message they sent in a group chat you are both in
       = their share ÷ others  a message you sent in a group chat they are in
worth  = its hours × catchUpHourWorth (50) × share
         (a trip's time together counts as F × √(hours ÷ F) past its first F = tripFullHours (8))
         (a day's answered calls with them count the same, by how long they were connected in all)
share  = s + (1 − s) ÷ n   with n people at it besides you, s = groupSharedPart (0.5); 1 for one person
```

Direct messages count by how evenly you both wrote, month by month (`directWeight` in `src/demos/closeness.ts`). A friendship is two-way: someone who sends you a stream of updates you stopped answering is not as close as a friend who writes as much as you do, however many messages there are. So each month's direct messages count 2 × √(yours × theirs): twice the geometric mean of the two sides, which is the plain count when you write as much as each other and less the more lopsided the month, either way round. Of 100 messages in a month, 50 each way count 100, 90 and 10 count 60, 99 and 1 count 20, and 100 from one side alone count nothing. `messageReciprocity` blends this with the plain count: 0 counts every message 1, as before; 0.5 counts 90 and 10 as 80. Each month is weighed on its own, before fading, so a friendship that went one-way lately counts less now while its even years still count as they were. Group chats keep their own rule (below), which already credits each side by how much the other writes, and so do chats where only your side is known (below), since there is nothing to weigh; catch-ups and calls are time together and count as they are.

A share is the share of that group's messages someone wrote, up to the month being scored. Each side's messages are credited by how active the other side is there, as a rough guess at how much of what each says is addressed to the other: for each member, a group adds (their messages × your share + your messages × their share) ÷ others. So a group you never write in adds nothing, and a member who never writes there gets nothing from yours either. "Others" is how many people are in the group besides you (archived people included): a message to a group is split among everyone in it, the way a direct message goes to the one other person, so no group message ever weighs more than a direct one. In a group of you and four others with 1,000 messages, where you write 200 (20%), a friend who also writes 200 gets (200 × 20% + 200 × 20%) ÷ 4 = 20, and a quieter one who writes 50 (5%) gets (50 × 20% + 200 × 5%) ÷ 4 = 5 (before fading): your messages are credited to each by how much they take part, not to everyone alike. Other members' messages to each other, Marketplace chats and system messages count nothing, and message text is never read: only who wrote where and when. The archive gives counts by month, so each message is dated at the middle of its month (in the month in progress, halfway through the part gone by), and a past month is scored as of its last day.

Some sources hold only your side: Discord's data package has only the messages you sent (its chats are marked `conversation.is_self_only`), so what anyone wrote back is not known, and scoring only yours would put Discord friends about half as close as they are. The score assumes an even exchange there. In a one-to-one chat, each of your messages counts 2, yours and a reply, an even exchange that balance never discounts (`directFromMeSelfOnly`, in the relationships report, counts them; the Table and the person panel still show the real counts, and the panel's Events chart draws the assumed replies as theirs, `directParts`). In a group chat, every other member is taken to have written as many messages as you did each month, so each of the n people in it, you too, holds a 1/n share, and the group gives each member (your messages × 1/n + your messages × 1/n) ÷ others (`selfOnlyGroupPoints`), the Events chart drawing half of it as theirs; a group there you never write in still adds nothing.

A hangout (a catch-up in the code and the archive; see "Hangouts" under "People" in the life archive's README) is time you had with someone in person that no message shows: a dinner, a wedding. It counts as its worth in direct messages on its date, dated at the middle of that day (today's counts whole), and fades by the same half-life, so an hour worth 50 counts 25 a half-life later, as 50 messages that day would. With several people there, each takes their share of its worth (`groupShare` in `src/catchUps.ts`): of n people (you not counted, the others there you did not name too), each takes s + (1 − s) ÷ n, s being `groupSharedPart` (0.5). Talk in a group is partly with everyone at once and partly with one person at a time, so an equal split (1/n, what the score did before) understates what each person there had of it; at half shared, nobody at a hangout gets under half of it. An hour alone with someone gives them 50, an hour with two people 37.5 each, two hours with four 62.5 each, and three hours with one friend and 2 others 100 for the friend (the others' shares count for nobody). Methodology's Decision history keeps the table this was chosen from. A **trip** (a hangout over several days, below) counts each of its days apart, dated that day and fading from there, as a hangout that day would: that day's time together, from when the trip started to when it ended, less the private hours (midnight to 10 am unless you say). Its time together counts whole for its first 8 hours (`tripFullHours`), then as 8 × √(hours ÷ 8) (`tripCountedHours` in `src/catchUps.ts`): a longer trip always counts for more, but each hour more adds less, so ten days away is not worth ten long days out. Each day takes its part of that by its time together (`catchUpCountedDayHours`), so each still fades from its own date. With one friend, a whole day away (14 hours) counts 529, a weekend (28) 748, five days (70) 1,183, ten days (140) 1,673 and a month (420) 2,898, where every hour whole would be 700, 1,400, 3,500, 7,000 and 21,000; a hangout on one date counts every hour whole, so a 14-hour day out is still 700. Methodology's Decision history keeps the table the curve was chosen from. Someone you have only hung out with, with no messages, has a score of their own and a place on the map. The defaults are set against the score itself:

- An hour is 50: about one day of real back-and-forth by text. Among your 50 most-messaged one-to-one chats over the last two years, a day with 20 or more messages averages about 49 both ways (the average day with any messages, 11). An hour together, on its own, puts someone with nothing else among your closest twenty or so on its day; a half-life on it still counts 25, and a year on about 3, still among the hundred the map names. A three-hour dinner counts 150.
- An hour in person and an hour on a call count the same: the score weighs how much time you spent, never how (a message counts 1 on every platform, a voice note or a sticker alike). The hours already say how long it was.

Calls from the phone's call history count without your adding them, and so do the calls you log yourself with Add event (see "Logged calls" under "People" in the life archive's README; one logged with several people adds each one's share of its time to their day, its unnamed others counted among them, the same share a hangout's worth is: an hour with three, at half shared, 40 minutes each, before the rest of this applies) and a WhatsApp three-way call (shared the same way among everyone on it, its unnamed others too; see "Calls" under "WhatsApp"): each day's answered calls with someone, added up, count as that many hours caught up with them that day, at the same worth an hour, once they reach `callMinimumMinutes` (5) (`callHours` in `src/catchUps.ts`): a 30-minute call is 25. Five minutes is past "I'm outside" and voicemail, which a call history counts as answered when it was you calling. A phone or FaceTime call you log with one person may be one the phone's call history brings in too, at its next import: on a day with both, the calls you logged with them so and those from the call history count whichever add up to longer, never both (`callDays` in the life archive's `server/archive/calls.ts`). A hangout the same day counts beside the calls (`callCatchUps` in `src/demos/closeness.ts`): a hangout is time in person, and a call is logged as a call. Missed calls count nothing, and calls never count as messages. Whether a call was answered is as its source says unless you said otherwise in the Table's Calls (its Answered select, kept in `call_answered`): one you say was missed counts nothing whatever its length, one you say was answered counts its length, and one you say is not known (empty) counts nothing. Messenger's, Instagram's, WhatsApp's, WeChat's and Telegram's calls in one-to-one chats count the same way, added to the day's phone calls with that person before `callMinimumMinutes` is applied (`callDays` in `server/archive/calls.ts`): their sources say how long each call was connected (kept as `message.call_seconds`; WeChat in the words its call record shows, "通话时长 12:05" or "Declined", read by `voipCall` in `server/connectors/wechat-ios/records.ts`; Telegram in the live bridge's notice, "Call ended (12 minutes)", read by `telegramCallNotice` in `server/connectors/matrix-bridge/rig.ts`). Every call record in a one-to-one chat counts as a call, not a message. One whose app does not say how long it was (WeChat, when it compressed the call's record and its dictionary was not given; Signal, which says only whether it was answered, so a missed one is 0 long; Messenger's phone-app export, which keeps a call as an empty link with nothing else) counts **nothing**, rather than a guess that would overstate closeness, until you give it a length: "Add length" beside it in the chat takes whole minutes (up to a day), and it then counts as a call that long, by the same rules. That length is yours (the `call_length` table, which imports never touch): "Edit" beside it changes it or removes it, it is one changeset like any edit (the chip's Undo, or the Table's Changes), and an app's own length can never be changed. "No answer" beside it instead says it was not answered (your answer, the `call_answered` table, as the Table's Calls' No): it then reads as a missed call and counts nothing, with "Remove" to take your answer away, one changeset too. A call in a group chat (where who was on the call is not known) still counts as one message there.

Everyone on the map is then ranked by their score, and the rank sets the distance. Whoever you tag `live-together` (a built-in tag, as `archive` and `keep` are) is infinitely close: ranked above everyone else every month, whatever their score, even none, and among themselves by their score (`closenessRanks`); Orbits sets them right next to the sun, in it, and the Table's closeness sort puts them first. Their score is still worked out and shown (the hover card, the Table). Take the tag off and they rank by their score again.

## How the trend is judged

How the trend is judged (`src/demos/trend.ts`): take how much you talked each month of the window (direct messages both ways plus what they sent in group chats), leaving out the month in progress, and fit a straight line through those months. If the line rises by at least half the window's average month from its first month to its last, you are **getting closer**; if it falls by that much, **drifting apart**; otherwise **steady**. With fewer messages than months in the window (under one a month), it is **too quiet to tell**.

## Reach-out glow

Everyone you should reach out to glows in their own colour on every map view (see Orbits in [The views](views.md)). How strongly is one slider, **Reach-out glow**, in the menu's settings (the title at the top left, then "<view> settings") on each view that draws the glow: Orbits, Orbits 3D, Circles, the Spider web and the City, last in each, set apart by a line. It is one setting for them all, so moving it on one view moves it on every one, and it is saved with the Spider web's settings (below), so every browser and the phone share it.

It reads **Usual** until it is moved, and the views look exactly as they always have; past it, it reads how far toward the strongest it is (+5% to +100%), and at the strongest (`glowLook` in `src/demos/reachGlow.ts`, which every view draws from):

- the glow round a marker reaches 2.2 times as far past it, its gradient up to full at its core (from 75%) and half halfway out (from 22%); the Spider web's halo and the City's beams (the beam over the roof, its spreading ring and a tower floor's pulse) grow and brighten alike;
- a glowing name's halo is a little stronger and gains a wider, softer one round it (`nameGlow` and `NAME_HALO` in `src/demos/orbitsLayout.ts`), outside the name's dark outline, so the letters stay sharp; Orbits 3D draws the same into its names once the view tilts;
- someone to reach out to is drawn, marker, trail and glow alike (their name fades by its place, as everyone's does; see [How names fade](#how-names-fade)), at no less than 85% strength, where usually they are never fainter than the last tier (40%), so a glow far out is not dimmed with its tier (`personOpacity`);
- past halfway the glows breathe, slowly (2.4 seconds in and out), growing up to 12% further and the names' wider halos 30% stronger at the top of each breath. Never with reduced motion (the system setting), and not Orbits 3D's glows in the scene, which it draws only as things move (its names from straight above breathe as Orbits' do); the City's beams breathe as well, over their rings' own pulse.

While someone is held (their panel open, not just under the pointer), only their glow and those of the people they share group chats with are drawn, whatever the setting; everyone else's is off until they are let go (see Orbits in [The views](views.md)).

Under No name except… (the name choice for showing your map to someone) no reach-out glow is drawn at all, held or not, and the setting lifts nobody: the views draw it as Usual with every glow off, until another name choice is made (see No name except… in [The views](views.md)).

The maps are drawn on a night sky in every theme, so each value is the same in light and dark.

## How names fade

On the map views (Orbits, Circles, the Spider web, Orbits 3D and the City), a name is drawn fainter the further down the ranking its person is. The **Names** group in each of their settings in the menu (after the view's own name settings; `src/demos/nameFade.ts`, the steps worked out by `fadeTiers` in `src/demos/orbitsLayout.ts`) sets how, one set for every view, and until it is moved the names are drawn as they always were, each as strong as its person's marker (`TIERS`):

- **Name fade from** (10, 25, 50, 100, 250 or 500; 10 until moved): how many of the closest keep their names at full strength.
- **Name fade to** (the same choices; 100 until moved): past how many of the closest names are at their faintest. Between the two, names step fainter at every guide ring (10, 25, 50, 100, 250, 500), each step the same: from 10 to 100 that is 100%, 80%, 60% and 40%, then the faintest. With it no further out than Name fade from, the closest are at full strength and everyone else at the faintest at once.
- **Faintest name** (0 to 100%; 20% until moved): how strong the faintest names are, past Name fade to and in the belt, and, while someone's panel is open, the names of everyone they share group chats with who is not named already (not on the City, which names nobody for a link alone). At 0 (Hidden) those names are not drawn at all, and their people keep their icons, as anyone unnamed does; at 100% no name fades.

Someone you should reach out to is named as everyone is: their name fades by their place (and, with Faintest name at Hidden, is not named past Name fade to), and the [Reach-out glow](#reach-out-glow) lifts their marker, not their name. There were once two more settings here, Reach-out names (whether those names stayed bright) and Linked names (how strong a held person's links' names were); a set that kept them (`name-fade.reach-out`, `name-fade.linked`) has them left out.

While someone is picked out (under the pointer, or their panel open), their name is at full strength and those they share group chats with at 70%, as before; everyone else's is at 20% (`BACKGROUND`), or fainter if their own fade is, so a name faded out stays out under the pointer. Only names follow these: markers, trails and glows keep `TIERS`. Show top names still says how many are named; these say how strongly. The three are saved on the server with the Spider web's settings (below), as `name-fade.top`, `name-fade.bottom` and `name-fade.faintest`.

## Look

The map views are drawn on the night sky, as they always were, or on paper: a light look, the life archive console's premium white and its inks, so friend-map embedded in the console (see [Inside the console](running.md#inside-the-console)) reads as one of its pages. **Look** in the open view's settings in the menu at the top left (after Name format; `src/theme.ts`, `LookSetting` in `src/ViewSettings.tsx`) is the console's own three choices: **Auto** (follow the device's own setting; the choice until one is made), **Light** and **Dark**. It is kept in this browser, the demo's apart, not on the server, since Auto is each device's own. While the address names a look (`?theme=light` or `?theme=dark`) or the page that embeds friend-map has set one, that wins while the page is open, and the menu says so in its place.

On paper every colour the night is drawn in has its ink: the tokens in `src/demos/demos.css` take Paper's values (`PAPER` in `src/demos/palette.ts`, beside the night's `COLORS`); Orbits' sun is a solid disc in its theme colour with a pale halo (`paperPalette` in `src/demos/orbitsTheme.ts`), the guide rings the same family darkened (`src/demos/ringColours.ts`), the Spider web drawn in ink with the haze white toward the edges (`paperCustomTheme` and Amber's paper theme in `src/demos/webTheme.ts`), the person panel the app's own white panel in the view's colours as ink (`src/demos/panelGlow.ts`), and every identicon the same picture in a pale background with its main colour as ink (`PAPER_LOOK` in `src/identicon.ts`), so names and glows, drawn in it, read on white. Since ink at an alpha reads fainter on white than light does on the night, every tier and floor of [How names fade](#how-names-fade), markers, trails and glows alike, is lifted a fifth of the way to full there (`PAPER_LIFT` in `src/demos/orbitsLayout.ts`), the closest at full strength still. Orbits 3D and the City are night scenes and stay dark as whole pages whatever the look, as do the Time river and the frozen views, whose own sheets are the night's alone (`night` in `src/views.ts`), and the Table and the Calendar for now.

## Saved view settings

The Spider web's settings (Name format, Show top names and Name padding, then Colour, Strand curve, Spokes, Web strength and Spider shadow; the names choice is one choice for every page), the Reach-out glow every glowing view shares and the map views' Names (see [How names fade](#how-names-fade); one set for every view) are kept on friend-map's server, in `data/view-settings.json`, not per browser, so the browsers at both addresses and the phone share one set. Each is kept as the page keeps a setting, under its name without `friend-map.`:

```json
{ "names": "initial", "web.spokes": "14", "web.curve": "0.1", "web.colour": "#65a5e2", "reach-glow": "0.6" }
```

Every page is served the set as it opens (a JSON script in its head, `server/savedSettingsPlugin.ts`, read afresh for each page), each choice is sent back a moment after it is made (`PUT /api/view-settings`, `src/savedStore.ts`), the default too, and a page coming back into view asks for the set again (`GET /api/view-settings`), so a choice made on another device shows there. A setting never chosen is missing, and is its default: the Spider web you settled on, First L., 14 spokes curved 10%, a blue and 5 px between names, with 35% web strength, the spider's shadow on and the closest 100 named, the usual Reach-out glow, and names fading as they always have. Only these settings, each one of its own choices or a number, are ever saved (`src/savedSettings.ts`; anything else is refused or left out, Name placement's `name-placement`, no longer kept, let pass unsaved), so the set is fit for the demo: the demo starts at it, live at `/demo` and built into the static demo when it is built (see [Demo](demo.md)), but for the few it has its own of (`DEMO_SAVED` in `src/demo/defaults.ts`), its visitors' own choices kept apart in their browsers. Settings the browsers kept before (`friend-map.web.spokes` and the like) are no longer read, and left in place. Orbits', the other views' and the Calendar's other settings are still per browser.
