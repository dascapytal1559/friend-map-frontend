# The views

A private map of everyone you talk to. It opens on the **Spider web** (`/` leads to `/spider`, unless the address names another view). The main view, **Orbits** at `/orbits`, puts you at the centre as the sun and everyone else around you: the closer someone is, the higher their closeness score (the messages between you, recent ones counting most, direct ones by how evenly you both write each month and group ones by how much you write there and how small the group is, and the hangouts and calls you have had with them; see [How closeness is scored](settings.md#how-closeness-is-scored)); the bigger, the more messages; a glow in their own colour marks everyone you should reach out to; and a comet trail behind someone runs from where they sat a year ago to now, warm if they moved in, cool if out. "Methodology" in the header writes all this out, with the current settings, and draws each rule that is a function as a small curve (what a lopsided month counts, how a message fades, what a day's calls count, each one's share of a group's time beside an equal split, what a trip counts by its length beside every hour whole, how far out each rank sits) that moves when the settings change; it opens as a wide panel on a computer and a full-screen sheet on a phone. People are spread evenly round the sun, A to Z clockwise from the top, and nothing else places them (no slices), and the **Circles** page at `/circles` shows the same map with people who share group chats side by side in friend circles (see "Orbits" under [The views](#each-view)).

Everyone you really talk with is shown, and everyone else is **archived**: hidden from the map (the Table's Hide, which the map follows, can show them again) and off the frozen map views, but still in People (under Archived) and the Table, searchable, with every message kept. Archiving is automatic for people you only share group chats with that are big or that you never wrote in, deleted accounts, Marketplace sellers and one-sided chats (see "Who is archived" under "The message archive" in the life archive's README), and yours to change: archive anyone by hand, or bring back someone the rules archived. Nobody is special: everyone is a person with a name, aliases, tags, a location (where they are, as far as you last knew), and a note. Hover anyone for the numbers behind them (and their location, when you have said); click to open their panel, which opens straight on who they are: their identicon beside their names as chips, the first their name and the rest their aliases (other names they go by; click one to change it, drag to reorder, and the **Names +** label turns into an "add new name" box; see "People" in the life archive's README), tags (what "archive" and "keep" do in the Tags label's tooltip), location and a note to edit, archiving, and their accounts (under "Accounts · 4", closed to start and remembered in this browser, with Join person at the heading's right either way; for someone joined by name the heading's tooltip says so) and fixes. Below them is Events (messages, calls and hangouts): a bar chart of how close you were each month from your first event to your last, in closeness points (what each month's events counted for in the score before fading, worked out by the same code and settings, see [How closeness is scored](settings.md#how-closeness-is-scored)): messages split by who wrote (what they sent rising above the line in solid grey, what you sent hanging below it as grey outlines, the month's balanced worth shared out in proportion; where a chat keeps only your side, as Discord's does, the reply the score assumes for each of yours is drawn as theirs, in the same grey; with "Show group messages" both sides add what their group messages counted for), and calls (a quiet green) and hangouts (a quiet sand), which are shared, half above and half below against the line, all on one scale, with a small legend under it and no caption; the month under the pointer, a tap or the arrow keys (with the chart focused) shows its points in a tooltip floating beside it, which takes no room at rest and goes on leaving, a tap elsewhere or Tab (screen readers hear the same) ("Mar 2024 · them 42 · you 18 · calls 25 · hangouts 100"; with assumed replies "them 12 (6 assumed)" and "Discord keeps only your side") and what made them up ("42 messages from them, 18 from you · 30 min of calls · 1 hangout"), their hangouts (time with them in person, under "Hangouts · 3", closed to start and remembered in this browser) with Add event at the heading's right, which adds a hangout or a call you log (see "Hangouts" and "Logged calls" under "People" in the life archive's README), the group chats they are in (under "Group chats · 3", closed to start and remembered in this browser; listed as a member or having written there, so even someone who never writes in one shows it: its platform, centred beside its name, then how many people, what they and you sent there and when it was last active, the latest first; one with no name at all, as an untitled iMessage group imports, is "-"; click one to open it, below; pointing at one, or moving into it with the keyboard, shows a link button at its right, "Not connected through this group", which unlinks them from it, and the row then keeps a broken link there, which connects them through it again, each a change with the chip's Undo; on a touch screen the button always shows, faintly, and the read-only demo has none; see "Unlinking someone from a group chat" under "The message archive" in the life archive's README), and their messages, laid out as a messaging app does (newest at the bottom, where the chat opens, in its own scroll box), loading older ones a page at a time as you scroll up, or all of them at once with "Load all" (inside Messages, on the line above the chat); their calls sit among the messages at their time ("Call · 3 h 12 min", "FaceTime video · 12 min", "Missed call"), on your side when you called, back as far as the messages loaded; the other apps' call records read the same way in place of the words their app wrote ("Call · 1 h 12 min", "Missed call", "Unanswered call", or just "Call" where the app does not say how long it was: a WeChat call whose record WeChat compressed (read only with WeChat's own dictionary), Signal's answered calls, Messenger's phone-app export, which keeps a call as an empty link, and a WhatsApp call answered on another of your devices; such a call in a one-to-one chat has "Add length" and "No answer" beside it, see [How closeness is scored](settings.md#how-closeness-is-scored); No answer says it was not answered, as the Table's Calls' No does, so it reads as a missed call marked "(yours)" and counts nothing, with Remove to take that away again, and the chip's Undo); and their hangouts and the calls you logged sit there too, each at its time among that day's messages and calls ("Hangout · 7:30 pm · 3 hours"), or at the start of its day when it has none ("Catch-up · 3 hours"), with who else was there and the note's first line: a click on one opens Add event's form on its kind, with Delete (its toggle makes a hangout a call or a call a hangout; see "Logged calls" under "People" in the life archive's README), and "+ event" beside a day's heading adds a call or a hangout on that day, each with the chip's Undo. The panel draws at once and loads the chart, the group chats and the chat after it, however long the history. A person's panel counts and shows your one-to-one chats with them; tick "Show group messages", inside Messages once it is opened (beside "Load all"), to add what they said in group chats you share (unticked on every page load, then kept from one panel to the next until the page is reloaded), where the name of the group each was said in opens that group. A group chat opens in Events in place of their chat, the rest of the panel staying as it is: its name (an untitled one is named by the first few people in it, most messages there first, as the Names choice shows them: "Ada, Bo and 3 more"; one with no name at all is "-"), platform, how many people, how many messages since when, everyone in it (click one to open their panel, and the arrow at the panel's top left goes back to whoever you opened them from, one person at a time, while someone opened from the map, the Table or the list, or closing the panel, starts afresh; those you have never changed are grey, not green, and the archived faint, see "Untouched" under "People" in the life archive's README), "Archive untouched" while anyone in it is untouched and not archived, and its messages, every sender named, opening at the newest and loading like any chat; "← Messages with …" goes back to their chat. In Accounts each account takes two lines, all the same height: its platform and name (cut short with "…" when too long, the whole name on hover), then, smaller, its messages and when the last was ("6452 msgs · 2026-08-21"), then its answered calls and their time when it has any (its app's, or ones you logged on it: "· 3 calls · 1 h 5 min"), each part kept whole; a call account, badged "Phone call", shows its answered calls (those you logged on it too), how long they were connected in all and when the last call was ("4 calls · 1 h 23 min · 2026-08-21"), with how many were not answered in its tooltip, or with none answered how many were missed ("1 missed call · 2026-08-21", "3 missed"). So does one account's own chat, opened with the chat icon beside it in Accounts (beside the × that is "Not them"), to see who an account really is before taking it off them: its platform and name, and only that account's messages, one-to-one and, with "Show group messages", what it said in groups (the timeline's `account=`). It changes nothing, so it is there even for a person with one account and on the read-only demo. Group chats are read only: friend-map sends only into one-to-one chats. While someone's panel is open they stay picked out on Orbits, with the people they share group chats with, as if under the pointer, so you can see where they sit and who they are linked to while you read; while one of their group chats is open in the panel, only the people in it are picked out with them (the group's members as its header lists them, so without dropped groups or members taken out; nobody, when it is one they are unlinked from), and going back to their chat, opening another group or closing the panel gives the rest back at once; pointing at someone else shows their hover card but keeps the open panel's person picked out, and closing the panel lets go. On the Table, the Calendar and the frozen map views the page's People button goes back to the list; the live map views (Orbits, Orbits 3D, Circles and the City) have no People button, to keep their header clean, and open a person's panel from the map alone.

**People** (the button on the Table, the Calendar and the frozen map views) opens everyone you have chatted with, built from a local message archive (Messenger, from the phone app's export and from Facebook's "Download your information", Instagram, WhatsApp and the rest; see "The message archive" in the life archive's README): name and tag people, give them aliases, archive or unarchive them, confirm or reject people who may be the same person, and open anyone's panel.

The **Table** (`/table`) lists everyone, archived people too, one row each, sortable by any column (location among them), or, on its Hangouts tab, every hangout, or on its Calls tab, every call from every source, newest first, with its length and a note to set, with the same person panel, filters and Names (First name, First L., Full name, No name or No name except…, in the menu at the top left) as Orbits; see [The views](#each-view) below.

The **Calendar** (`/calendar`) lays out every day: a month, each day shaded by how many messages it had and marked with its hangouts and calls; click a day for everything on it, its hangouts, calls (those you logged too) and chats with their messages, one-to-one only until you include group messages, and Add event there adds one on that day; see [The views](#each-view) below.

The **Time river** (`/time-river`) shows your hangouts, calls (or both together) or messages over time in the river's look: time runs left to right and each person is a stream as wide as that month's hours together, minutes on calls or messages, so you can see who you saw, called or wrote to when; a bar on the page chooses which, over what time and on which app; see [The views](#each-view) below.

The title at the top left of every page is the view switcher: click it, or the caret beside it, to reach the main views (Orbits, Orbits 3D, the Spider web and the City, listed first as a group of their own), the Table, the Calendar, the Time river, the demo at `/demo` (the map views on a made-up crowd, for showing it to someone: the same pages as the public demo, see [Demo](demo.md)) and the **frozen views**: the river and Reciprocity (the tldraw canvas that was at `/canvas`, Constellations and Friend galaxies are retired; their addresses lead to Orbits). They keep working on the same data but get no new features; see [The views](#each-view) below. A view's settings are in the same menu, in a section set apart under "<view> settings", a heading that folds it away or opens it (closed to start, and kept per browser, one choice for every view) (`src/viewSettings.ts` declares each view's, `src/ViewSettings.tsx` draws them, toggle buttons for a choice between a few and a slider for a range, its value beside it): first, on every page that shows names, **Name format** (see Orbits below), then Orbits' **Theme**, the Spider web's **Colour**, **Strand curve**, **Spokes** and **Web strength**, the Calendar's **Messages**, and each main view's own **Show top names**, how many of the closest it names without hovering (25, 50, 100, 200 or All; 100 on every main view until another is chosen, 50 in the demo; `src/demos/topNames.ts`), which never moves anyone nearer the sun or further, and on Orbits and the Spider web **Name padding**; nothing of them is on the page itself. The Spider web's come in two groups, set apart by a line: its name settings together (Name format, Show top names, Name padding), then its look (Colour, Strand curve, Spokes, Web strength). Those seven, the names choice included, are **saved on friend-map's server** rather than per browser, so every browser and the phone share one set, and the demo starts at them (see [Saved view settings](settings.md#saved-view-settings)); the rest are kept per browser. A small local record of which views you open (`npm run usage`) shows which are worth keeping.

## Each view

Every view reads the same data and shows everyone who is not archived. Hovering a person shows the numbers behind them, and clicking opens their panel, where "Archive" takes them off every view at once. The title at the top left of every page (the view's name, with a caret beside it) is the switcher: clicking either, or Enter or Space on it, lists them, then the Demo (`/demo`, see "The demo" under Orbits) under "About the app", and Esc closes the list (`src/ViewSwitcher.tsx`):

- **Orbits** at `/orbits`, the main view. All new view work goes here. Its **Theme**, in Orbits' settings in the menu, colours the sun and the guide rings: Orange, the sun's own, or Custom, any colour from a colour picker, its lightness kept where it reads on the night sky, the rings' gradient starting at it and turned round the wheel with it; kept per browser (`friend-map.orbits.theme`, the last custom colour under `friend-map.orbits.customColour`; `src/demos/orbitsTheme.ts`), and Orbits' alone (Circles and Orbits 3D keep the orange). Distance is closeness (see [How closeness is scored](settings.md#how-closeness-is-scored)), by rank: the closest person sits next to the sun and everyone else steps outward in order, on an eased log scale of the rank (whoever is tagged `live-together` sits in the sun itself, inside the closest orbit, at `HEARTH` of its radius, evenly spaced round it from the direction the first of them would otherwise take, A to Z or by circle, so one alone keeps their own direction; the rest are placed as if they were not there, so tagging someone moves nobody else; `hearth` in `src/demos/orbitsLayout.ts`), so the closest few have room while hundreds from years ago still fit; guide rings mark the closest 10, 25, 50, 100, 250 and so on, labelled "top 10", "top 25" and so on, as far as there are people to fill them, each ring and its label in one colour of a gradient that starts at the sun's orange and cools through amber and dusky rose to a muted violet, fainter ring by ring out to the top 500, the labels faded so they do not compete with the names (`src/demos/ringColours.ts`; the same on Circles, Orbits 3D and the City's avenues). People with no closeness yet sit in the outer belt. Size is messages overall, except that the hundred closest are drawn at least big enough to read their picture (`READABLE` in `src/demos/orbitsLayout.ts`, a radius of 9 pixels). Everyone is drawn as their identicon (see "Identicons" under "People" in the life archive's README), a round marker filled with it as crypto wallets show one (the corners fall outside the circle); it means nothing else. Each name on the map is a soft tint of its person's identicon main colour, halfway from it to the plain light text in OKLab (`nameColour` and `NAME_TINT` in `src/demos/orbitsLayout.ts`), so where markers crowd together each name is easy to match to its own, while it still reads on the night sky at better than 10:1 (tests check its hue, colour and contrast over thousands of seeds). Each identicon is one SVG pattern the map defines once, so drawing it for everyone costs no more than a flat dot did: measured on demo crowds of 90 and 700 in Chrome, zooming and hovering ran at the same frame times either way, and a whole drawing of 700 people took 98.5 ms against 95 ms before. Strength is closeness too: everyone, their marker, name, trail and glow alike, is drawn fainter tier by tier along the guide rings, the closest 10 at full strength, the 11th to 25th at 80%, the 26th to 50th at 60%, the 51st to 100th at 40%, and everyone from the 101st on, and the belt, at 20% (`TIERS` and `TIER_FLOOR` in `src/demos/orbitsLayout.ts`, one table to tune), so nothing of a person outshines them. A glow marks everyone you should reach out to ("Should reach out" in their panel, see "Reaching out" under "People" in the life archive's README; `glowOf` in `src/demos/orbitsLayout.ts`), in their own identicon main colour (`glowColour`), with a soft halo of it round their name outside its dark outline, so the letters stay sharp (`nameGlow` and `NAME_GLOW`, a CSS drop-shadow drawn only on the names of those glowing; each glowing person has a gradient of their own, drawn only for them, so the glow costs nothing for everyone else), and fades in or out as you switch it on or off; they are always named, wherever they rank, and they, their name, trail and glow are never fainter than the last tier, read from the table itself, so they keep a higher tier of their own (`personOpacity`). How strongly they glow is the **Reach-out glow** in the menu's settings, one setting for Orbits, Orbits 3D, Circles, the Spider web and the City (see [Reach-out glow](settings.md#reach-out-glow)). Nobody's edge comes nearer than the sun's. The hundred closest are named (the closest fourteen a little larger), every one of them, each name as strong as its person; on a phone (the phone layout, `PHONE` in `src/demos/Orbits.tsx`, see [From your phone](running.md#from-your-phone)) nobody but whoever is picked out. Whoever is picked out (the person whose panel is open, else anyone under the pointer) is named too, and they are drawn at full strength and the people they share group chats with at 70%, whatever their tiers, while everyone else is drawn at one flat 20%, whatever their tier (`BACKGROUND` beside `TIER_FLOOR`); while someone is held (their panel is open, not just under the pointer), everyone they share group chats with who is in view is named too, closest first, those not named already at 40% (`LINKED_FLOOR` beside `TIER_FLOOR`), so the people far out stay readable, and the lines to them are drawn at 40% too; while one of their group chats is open in their panel, all this goes only to the people in it (`heldGroup` in `ViewProps`, `linksOf` in `src/demos/Orbits.tsx`), the rest drawn at 20% with everyone else; each given a name, in place of their icon (No name still names nobody); names and trails wait until someone has reached their place, so they never pile up while people rise out of the sun or move. **Your photo**: hovering the sun (or tapping it on a phone) shows a "+" that opens a file picker; a JPEG, PNG, WebP or HEIC photo of up to 15 MB is then drawn in place of the sun, round, with no text, the sun's glow still round it and a ring in your colour. Hovering it again shows "+" to change it and a small "×" to remove it, back to the sun. The server keeps it as `data/me/photo.<ext>`, replacing any before it, and knows what it is from its bytes, never its name; a HEIC photo (an iPhone's) is kept as a JPEG, made by macOS's `sips`, since only Safari shows HEIC (`server/mePhoto.ts`, `src/demos/SunPhoto.tsx`). The demo and the static demo never ask for it and offer no "+": their sun is always the sun; so is the Spider web's (below). **Zoom**: the mouse wheel or a trackpad pinch zooms in and out about the pointer, dragging empty space pans, and the + (or =), − and 0 keys when not typing zoom about the middle of the map and go back to the whole map; on a touch screen two fingers pinch to zoom about them and one drags to pan (see [From your phone](running.md#from-your-phone)). Zooming spreads people out from the sun while the sun, names, ring labels and the hover card keep their size, and a marker smaller than `READABLE` grows with the zoom until it reaches it, never faster than the room around it (`drawnSize`), so the identicons of the people zoomed in on can be read; so zooming opens room between people, up to twelve times, enough to part the crowd by the sun and the long tail on the outer orbits. It never zooms out past the whole map, and pans only so far that the system's edge never comes inside where it sits unzoomed. Zoomed in, more people are named: the room grows as the square of the zoom, and so does the count, the next closest people in view after the hundred filling it (300 names at most), each in place of its person's icon and as strong as its person, while the hundred closest keep theirs whenever they are in view; names are placed again once the zoom holds still. Hover, clicking someone, the picked-out highlighting, trails and glows work at any zoom; people off the page are not drawn, and anyone passing under the header fades out under it. The zoom is kept while the page is open, through switching views and new queries, unless a new query would leave nobody in view, when it goes back to the whole map; a reload starts unzoomed. Its maths is in `src/demos/orbitsZoom.ts`. **Find**: the header's "Find a person" box (`src/demos/FindBox.tsx`) finds people on the map as the Table's search does, by name, alias, note or location (`src/demos/find.ts`, which both share; the map's report carries each person's note for it), and lists the first eight, closest first, then the belt, most talked to first, with their rank. Pick one with a click, or the arrow keys and Enter, and their panel opens and they are picked out, as a click on them does; if they are off the page, or too small to read their identicon, the map glides to them, centred, zoomed in until it can be read (`zoomToShow`), and keeps them in view as their panel docks over the map, until you zoom or pan. Escape clears the box. It finds only who is on the map: someone the query or Hide leaves off (the archived, until Hide shows them) is found in the Table. A person is labelled, here and in People, by their name, the first of the names they go by (`src/names.ts`); the hover card and People show their next name, their first alias, beneath the label where they have one. **Name format**, the first of the view's settings in the menu the title opens at the top left (on every live view, the Table and the Calendar, and on the demo's live views; `src/NamesMenu.tsx`, `src/names.ts`), a couple of clicks away so the header stays clean for a screenshot, is one choice for every page, not one per view, a toggle as the other settings are, saved on the server as `names` (`first`, `initial`, `full`, `hide` or `except`; see [Saved view settings](settings.md#saved-view-settings)), so the h key's No name holds on every browser too; the demo's visitor's apart, in their browser under `friend-map.names@demo`. Four of its choices are for the map alone, the names drawn on Orbits, Circles, Orbits 3D, the Spider web and the City: **First name** ("Ada" for Ada Sample), **First L.** (the first name and the first letter of the last, with a full stop: "Ada S."), **Full name** and **No name**, which names nobody on the map, for a screenshot of its shape, say, and which the h key, when not typing, also switches on and back to the choice before. They shorten the label each person already has (their name), never a stored name: only words with a letter in them count, so an emoji or a number beside a name is no word of it, and a name with fewer than two such words, one in Chinese, Japanese or Korean (spaced or not), an email or a platform's name for a deleted account ("Facebook user") is drawn as it is. They change nothing else: the hover card, People, the person panel (its name chips, which you change, among them), search, the Table, the Calendar and chats name everyone in full whichever of them is on, and Show top names still say how many the map names. Where the map draws names is not a setting: it was (Name placement: next to the icon, on it, or in its place), until you settled on names in place of icons. On the map views (Orbits, Orbits 3D, Circles and the Spider web) each named person is drawn as their name alone, centred where their icon would be (`centreOnIcons` in `src/demos/orbitsLayout.ts`; on Orbits and the Spider web those named on the whole map are first turned round their orbits, the closest keeping their place and the rest moving to the nearest angle where their name, measured as drawn in the view's own type, covers no other name and no ring label (on the Spider web, where it sits on its ring's level top thread), never more than about 20°, so nobody's distance from the sun changes; each name kept clear of the others and of the ring labels, across and up and down, ink to ink, by that view's **Name padding**, a slider in Orbits' and the Spider web's own settings, 0 to 10 pixels, until moved 3 on Orbits (the room the names had before it) and 5 on the Spider web, kept per view: Orbits' per browser under `friend-map.orbits.name-padding`, the demo's apart, and the Spider web's saved on the server as `web.name-padding` (`src/demos/namePadding.ts`), a name with no angle that clear within reach taking the one where it covers least; Circles and Orbits 3D leave them where they are, piling up as they will), with no icon, glow or halo under it, while everyone not named (beyond Show top names, or with No name) keeps their icon, so nobody leaves the map; the name takes the icon's part: the pointer or a finger on it hovers, opens and holds them (the views pick the person whose name is under the pointer themselves, `nameAt` in `src/demos/Orbits.tsx`), and on the Spider web the beam and the hold end at its edge, the reticle and the boot's bracket go round it, and the web runs on behind it, with nothing cut out of it, the name's dark outline keeping it readable (the scene's `names` in `src/demos/webLook.ts`). Each names the same people, as Show top names says, closest first, and leaves none of them out; whoever is picked out is always named, drawn over the rest. Each name is drawn in its person's own icon colour, with the dark outline every name has and nothing behind it, no card on any view, and the halo of someone to reach out to round its letters. On Orbits 3D tilted, each name sits in its person's place at their depth; the City paints names on roofs, and the tower floors' names on their floors. The fifth, **No name except…**, in the real app alone, is a privacy mode, for showing your real map to someone: it hides every name on every screen you can click to but those of the people you pick, until you choose another. Under it the menu lists whom it names, each with a × to stop naming them, and a box to name someone else, by name or alias, from everyone (archived people last); they are tagged `show-name` (a built-in tag, see "People" in the life archive's README), so a pick is kept in the archive, the same in every browser, and goes in the change log as any tag change does (Undo in the Table's Changes takes it back); tagging someone `show-name` in their panel or with the CLI picks them too, and it follows a merge as any tag does (`src/shownNames.ts` loads them, with `GET /api/archive/people?tag=show-name`, while the choice is on; nobody is named until they are in). Their connections are named with them: everyone on the map a picked person shares a group chat with, the people the map links them to while they are held (by the members each group chat connects, so not through one someone is unlinked from, and nobody archived; `connectionsOf` in `src/shownNames.ts`, from the map's `GET /api/archive/relationships`, loaded beside them, so until it is in only those picked are named). They are not picked: the menu lists only those picked, under them a line saying everyone they share a group chat with is named too, and nobody is tagged. Those picked and their connections are named by their name alone, everywhere; everyone else is unnamed: the map names nobody else, and wherever else a name would be (the hover card, People and its proposals, the person panel and its name chips, group chats and their members, message senders and reactions, the Table's rows and hangouts, Changes, the Calendar's day) there is nothing, or a faint bar where something must be there to click, so every row still opens; a group chat has no name, a Marketplace chat no seller, one-to-one messages no sender, and an account only its platform. It only ever takes names away: each view still names whom it would anyway (on Orbits, the Spider web and Orbits 3D as many of the closest as each one's Show top names says, on Circles the closest 100, more as you zoom in, up to 300 or as many as are named, if more; everyone to reach out to; whoever is pointed at or picked out; and while someone's panel is open, everyone they share a group chat with), and of those only the names of those picked and their connections show, so nobody else is named, even when pointed at, and nobody takes their place. So on the map a connection is named as anyone is, if they are among the closest Show top names says (or to reach out to, or pointed at), and open a picked person's panel (click them, which on the Spider web holds them) and all their connections are named, however far out, until it closes (not on a phone, which names only whoever is picked out; only the members of a group chat open in it, while one is, as its links are). Search follows it: the find box, the Table's search and sorting by name, its hangouts, People's search, Join person and adding someone to a hangout find only those named, by their name. Aliases, the next name beneath a label, notes and draft openers hide too, and the name fields lock; tags (and the friend circles' tag names) show; you (the sun, or "you" atop the City's tower) are never named but as "You", as under every choice, and your own accounts' names hide. While it is on, a small dot beside the settings' title in the menu says so, folded or not, and nothing on the page itself. Hovering a stream or a mark shows who, that month's amount (and missed calls), all of it over the span, and their busiest month; clicking opens their panel, docked beside it, and they stay picked out until it closes. It is a live view: the Query bar, the Table's Hide (archived people only once their box is unticked) and the find box narrow and find who it shows, as on Orbits, and the people shown with none of what it measures are counted in the key, not drawn. The demo has no Time river.
- **Frozen views**: the river (`/river`) and Reciprocity (`/reciprocity`). They are kept working but not extended. When a change would break one, it is not fixed by default: you decide, with how often it is used (`npm run usage`). Any view unopened for 30 days is proposed for removal, never removed without a yes. Retired, at the user's word: the tldraw canvas (`/canvas`), with the person files, its saved layout, `data/canvas.json`, left on disk unread; and Constellations (`/constellations`) and Friend galaxies (`/galaxies`). Their addresses lead to Orbits.

Addresses from before (`/demos`, `/demos/<slug>`) still work and are sent to where the views are now.

## What the views read

The Table reads `GET /api/archive/table?closenessHalfLifeDays=&catchUpHourWorth=&callMinimumMinutes=&messageReciprocity=&groupSharedPart=&tripFullHours=` (the page's settings; the default for any left out, and 400 for anything else) (`PeopleTable` in `src/archive.ts`), which friend-map's server answers itself (`server/methodology.ts`) from the archive's rows (`GET /api/archive/table-rows`, `TableRow`) and its score report (below): one light row per person, archived people too, with their name, aliases, tags ("archive" added for everyone the rules archive, `archived` saying it was a rule), note, why they are archived, the platforms of their accounts, their counts and last message, the same counts and last message on each platform (`byPlatform`, for the Platform picker), their calls added up (`calls`: answered, not answered, seconds connected and when the last call was, or null with none; `callCounts` in `server/archive/calls.ts`), and their closeness score now with those settings (`closenessNow` in `src/demos/closeness.ts`, the same score the map computes, catch-ups included), without accounts or months. It never returns message text. The archive keeps no copy of the scoring: it gives the facts, and friend-map scores them. `GET /api/archive/score-report` (`ScoreReport` in `src/archive.ts`; `structure.scoreReport()`) is everyone's messages by month, the group chats, the catch-ups and the calls, with anyone at a catch-up or on a call but without messages as someone with no months; from it friend-map's server also answers the person panel's activity chart, `GET /api/archive/people/:id/activity?<settings>` (`personActivity` in `src/activity.ts`). The score report and the rows carry an ETag made of the archive's people and links versions (which change with everything either holds) and of the server process, and answer 304 to an If-None-Match naming it, and friend-map reads them over HTTP from the archive's server (`server/archiveClient.ts`), keeping each by its ETag and asking again with If-None-Match, so it reads them again only once the archive has changed. A change to the scoring in `src/` reaches the Table and the activity chart only once friend-map's server restarts, while the map views, which score in the page, show it at once.

The Calendar reads `GET /api/archive/calendar/:month` (`CalendarMonth` in `src/archive.ts`; `server/archive/calendar.ts`): each local day of the month (cut at its own midnight, whatever the clocks' offset) with each chat's messages that day, its calls and its catch-ups, everyone in each by person id (a group's listed members, less anyone taken out, and whoever wrote there that month), those people's tags ("archive" for the rules' archive too) for Hide, and the first and last days with anything in the archive; `GET /api/archive/calendar/:day` (`CalendarDay`): the day's catch-ups, calls and chats in time order, each chat with its messages that day, its first and last, its size and the first few people of an untitled group to name it by, and everyone in them; and one chat's messages that day, from the content path, `GET /api/archive/content/conversations/:id/days/:day?after=&limit=` (oldest first, up to 5000 a page). A month is counted in the database from the `message_time` index (migration 30: every message by when it was sent, with its chat, sender and type), so it reads only that month's rows: on a made-up archive of a million messages a busy month (14,000 messages) answers in about 4 ms, against 7 s without the index, and its busiest day (2,100 messages) in about 20 ms. The structure routes never return message text; anything but a month or a day is a 400.

The map views draw from one structure-only route, `GET /api/archive/relationships` (`RelationshipsReport` in `src/archive.ts`), and Orbits narrows it by the ids the Query bar's `GET /api/archive/query` matches. It holds only the people on the map; archived people, most of the archive, are only counted (`archivedCount`; the Table lists them), though group chats still name them so a group keeps its real size. With `reveal=archive` (archive unticked in the Table's Hide, which the map follows) and the query that ran as `q`, it also holds the archived people the query matches, archived by you or the rules, and only those, so the map stays light until you ask for them; Orbits asks for it only once the Query bar has counted the query under Hide within its limit of 1,000 (see [The Query bar](#the-query-bar)). Per person: name, tags (with "archive" for the rules' archive too), whether the account is unknown, the platforms of their accounts, messages by sender (`directFromMe`, `directFromThem`, `groupFromMe` for what you sent in group chats they are in, `groupFromThem`; system messages never count), first and last message, the same counts per local month, reactions each way, and `openings`: direct conversations picked up again after 14 or more quiet days, by who wrote first. Plus every group chat as the person ids in it and how busy it is, with per local month its message count, yours, and each person's who wrote (for the closeness score), without its title; and every catch-up, its day, kind, way and the ids of everyone at it (`catchUps`, read fresh each time, for the closeness score). It never returns message text.

## How a map view is built

How a map view is built, in `src/demos/` (the folder keeps its name from when these were demos):

- Each view is one file (`Orbits.tsx`, `River.tsx`, `Reciprocity.tsx`) exporting a `Demo`: its slug, title, one-line description shown under it (the main views have none: Methodology explains them), and a `View` component. `Demos.tsx` lists them in `DEMOS`, and `src/views.ts` gives every view its path, title and whether it is frozen. `Placeholder.tsx` is the smallest working example of a view. The Table is not a `Demo` (it draws no model): `TablePage.tsx` is its page, `Table.tsx` the table, `table.ts` its filtering and sorting, `tablePlatform.ts` its remembered platform, `tableRows.ts` its tab (people, catch-ups or calls, in the address), `AddEvent.tsx` its Add hangout and Add call, and `CatchUpsTable.tsx` and `catchUpsTable.ts` its catch-ups; `page.tsx` holds what it shares with `Demos.tsx` (the docked person panel, a switch remembered per browser, the Names choice, the error notice).
- A `View` gets `ViewProps` (`view.tsx`): the `model`, the `width` and `height` to draw at (the stage's: the whole window, less a docked panel; while the panel slides, the bigger of where the stage was and where it is headed, so the drawing, centred, always covers it, `mapSize.ts`; leave `inset` pixels at the top for the header), or, on a view that keeps its size (`keepsSize` on its `Demo`: Orbits, Circles and the Spider web), always the whole window, with `seen`, how much of it across is in view: the docked panel opens over the map, whose stage slides aside by half the panel on a CSS transform, so what the panel leaves is centred and the map is never drawn again for the panel (only the compositor moves it), and Orbits pans, just far enough, to keep whoever's panel is open in view beside it (`panToShow` in `orbitsZoom.ts`), its zoom's bounds letting the system's edges come into view (`boundsOf`) and gliding back within the whole map's as the panel closes; `hover(tip | null)` to show the hover card, `open(person)` to open the person panel, and `held`, whose panel is open: Orbits (`OrbitMap`) picks them out while nobody is under the pointer (`focusOf` in `Orbits.tsx`), and the frozen views leave it; `heldGroup`, the group chat open in their panel (which the person panel tells the page through `onGroup`), to which Orbits then narrows their links (`linksOf`); and `find`, whoever was last picked in the find box, whom Orbits brings into view. The page hands it over deferred (`useDeferredValue`), so a panel opens before the map redraws. The page (`Demos.tsx`) owns the route, header (the title, which is the view switcher, Orbits' find box, Methodology, Names, People, and the Query bar with Hide at its end), hover card, the People and person panel (which it hands what the model knows of a person, so their panel shows them at once), errors from edits the archive refused, and the usage record, and reloads the model whenever people change, here or from the CLI (`followArchive` in `src/useArchive.ts`).
- `closeness.ts` is the closeness score: `closenessScores` (every person's score as of each month, from their direct messages, weighed month by month by `directWeight`, and the group chats' monthly counts), `closenessRanks`, and the rule written out at the top of the file. `Methodology.tsx` is the Methodology panel that explains it on the page, drawing each rule that is a function with `Curve.tsx`.
- `model.ts` turns the report into a `DemoModel`: the people shown, every month from the first message or catch-up to now, the settings it was built with (`settings`: the trend window and the closeness settings), and per `DemoPerson` their totals, `closeness` and `rank` per month, your share of the direct messages, openings, reactions, a `series` of messages per month (direct both ways plus their group messages), and `community`, a friend group found from shared group chats (used by the frozen views; Orbits places no one by it). `links` joins people who share group chats, weighted so a big group binds each pair less. `standing(person, month, model)` gives total, days since they last talked (exact now, by month in the past) and the three-month trend as of any month; `trendAt(person, month, model)` gives the longer trend over the window (`trend.ts`, which holds the rule), which the hover card gives.
- `orbitsSky.ts` is what Orbits and Orbits 3D share of the sky: `stageOf` (the sun's spot and the orbits' reach for a stage), `useOrbitBodies` (where everyone sits round the sun, A to Z or in friend circles, with their standing, place by closeness, trend and where their trail starts), `useOrbitNames` (which names are placed where, for wherever a view draws people), and `useTween` (how people move to new places, and rise out of the sun at first, or, on the Spider web, come in with its scan). A change to where someone sits or who is named is made once, here, for both views.
- `orbitsLayout.ts` is Orbits' geometry: closeness rank to orbit and the rings, `directions` (the even, name-ordered spread), `placeBodies` (pushing apart until nobody overlaps, comparing only near neighbours through a grid, so hundreds of people place in milliseconds), `namedIds` (who is named without hovering), `fitNames` and `centreOnIcons` (names turned clear of each other, then centred in their people's places), and the comet trails (`trailStartRank`, where someone sat at the start of the window, and `displacementTrail`, the tapering outline from where someone sat at the start of the window to now).
- `orbitsZoom.ts` is Orbits' zoom: a `Zoom` spreads the layout's places out from the sun by `k` and shifts them, `clampZoom` keeps it between the whole map and twelve times and the system's edge outside where it sits unzoomed, `zoomAt` zooms about a point (the pointer, or the middle), `panBy` follows a drag, `wheelFactor` turns a wheel event or pinch into a factor, `zoomToShow` and `zoomBetween` bring someone found into view and glide there, and `chooseNamed` picks who is named at a zoom.
- `view.tsx` also has `basicRows`, `fieldRows` (location) and `trendLabel` for hover cards, `topIds` to pick whom to label, `Legend` and `Swatch`. `palette.ts` has the colours and `demos.css` the same as CSS variables with the type: serif for titles and names, system-ui otherwise. Put a view's own styles in its own stylesheet.
- d3 is available (scales, shapes, `d3-force`); the map views use SVG, an HTML canvas, or, for Orbits 3D's spheres, WebGL written by hand (no library).

## Orbits 3D

**Orbits 3D** at `/orbits-3d` is Orbits as a solar system (`src/demos/Orbits3D.tsx`). It opens exactly as Orbits does, seen from straight above: the same people in the same places, the sun, the guide rings, the glows, the comet trails and the names, because both views place everyone through the same hooks (`src/demos/orbitsSky.ts`: the stage, `useOrbitBodies` and `useOrbitNames`), and the camera is orthographic, so a height changes nothing until the view tilts. Then the camera moves as the City's does (`src/demos/cityCamera.ts`): a drag with the middle or right mouse button, or with Ctrl held, turns the sky round the sun as it goes sideways and tilts it as it goes up (further over, to 70°) or down (back toward straight above); on a touch screen two fingers twisted turn it and two fingers dragged up or down tilt it, while a pinch still zooms, each past a small deadzone. The compass in the bottom-left corner shows the bearing and the lean and glides the camera back to straight above, north up, where the view is Orbits again; `0` resets the zoom alone, and both are kept while the page is open, as Orbits' zoom is.

For recording a clip of the view, `?tour=` in the address plays a scripted camera move as the page opens (`src/demos/orbits3dTour.ts`): the sky arrives from straight above, as Orbits, holds a beat, then the camera leans down to a low angle, fast in the middle with a long ease-out, and settles. `tilt` leans alone (to 62°) and holds still; `orbit` leans while turning 40° round the sun and zooming in a little, then drifts on, turning 3° a second; `push` leans to 56° with a smaller turn while zooming in twice as far over 7 seconds, and drifts on slowly. It runs on the page's own animation frames, so a recorder that steps the page's clock gets the same move every time; any turn, zoom or pan stops it, as it stops a glide, and under reduced motion the view goes straight to where the lean ends. Without `?tour=` nothing changes. The path is tested in `src/demos/orbits3dTour.test.ts`: each tour holds straight above until its hold is over, leans no further than its angle, never jolts from one frame to the next, and drifts on at its own speed.

As the view tilts, each disc rounds into a sphere lit by the sun at the middle (so a body on the near side of the sun shows a crescent, as a planet does), with its identicon painted on its top, where it foreshortens with the tilt and the underside shows the identicon's background colour; the shading fades in over the first 25° of tilt (`shadeAt` in `src/demos/orbits3dScene.ts`), so from straight above the spheres are Orbits' flat discs. Each person's orbit leans a little out of the sun's plane, up to 14° (`INCLINE_MAX`), by an inclination and a node taken from their identicon seed (`inclinationOf`), so a person always leans the same way and a reload never reshuffles the sky; their height is that of a body on an inclined circular orbit at their direction (`heightOf`), the belt and whoever sits in the sun staying in the plane. So the disc opens into a system with depth, the guide rings become ellipses in the plane, the trails rise from the plane to their people, and the sun, the spheres and the glows are painted from the back to the front as the camera sees them (`paintOrder`, by `nearness`; from straight above, where everyone is equally near, in Orbits' own order). The names move into the scene (`src/demos/orbits3dNames.ts`): from straight above they are Orbits' own, flat, each in its person's place; once the view tilts past 2° they fade over the next 6° (`namesInScene`) into names standing in their people's places, facing the camera, drawn on the WebGL canvas at their person's depth, so a sphere or the sun's middle nearer the camera hides a name behind it and a name never shows through a sphere in front; who is named follows the same rule as Orbits (the Name format choice, hover and the held person's emphasis, and on a phone only whoever is picked out), the names are placed for the tilted positions once the view holds still, and each name is drawn once, with Orbits' type, outline and glow at the screen's own pixel density, into one texture (`NameAtlas`), drawn again only when it changes; the ring labels sit at the top of each ellipse.

Everything else is Orbits': hovering shows the card, clicking opens the panel, whoever's panel is open stays picked out with the lines to the people they share group chats with and everyone else dimmed, the find box brings someone into view, and the Query bar, Hide, Methodology, Names (the labels follow the choice, as on Orbits), the settings and the demo (`/demo/orbits-3d`) apply; it zooms and pans the same way (`src/demos/orbitsZoom.ts`), the zoom's bounds following the system's reach as the camera sees it (`sceneExtent`). Your photo, when one is set, is drawn in place of the sun as on Orbits, and its "+" and "×" work the same.

It is drawn on three layers: an SVG beneath for what lies in the sun's plane (the rings, the trails, the lines to whoever is picked out), a WebGL canvas for the sun, the glows, the spheres and, tilted, the names (`src/demos/orbits3dGl.ts`; the solid things write their depth as they are painted back to front, the glows neither test nor write it, and the names test it, one draw call per run of a kind), and an SVG above for the text that stays flat (the ring labels, and from straight above the names), which keeps Orbits' own type and styles. WebGL because a lit sphere is per-pixel work that only a shader does cheaply for hundreds of spheres every frame while the camera turns, on a phone too; it is written by hand in plain WebGL 2 (a few hundred lines, instanced, one draw call a frame, everyone's identicons in one texture atlas), with no library, so the view adds a few kilobytes rather than a 3D engine, and it is loaded only when opened (`Demos.tsx` loads it lazily), so no other view carries it. The canvas is drawn at no more than 2× the screen's pixels. A browser without WebGL 2 is told so on the stage, and Orbits itself shows the same map. Its maths is tested on made-up people in `src/demos/orbits3dScene.test.ts`: from straight above every position equals Orbits' at every zoom whatever the heights, the heights come from the seed alone and lean each orbit as an inclined circle does, the painting order, the extent holding every leaning body, the light, the camera's bounds and the compass's glide landing exactly. The names' rules are tested in `src/demos/orbits3dNames.test.ts`: nothing moves into the scene from straight above or through a nudge, everything past the threshold, smoothly between; the depth given to each sprite hiding a name behind a nearer sphere and showing one in front, flat from straight above; and the passes keeping the painting order.

## City

**City** at `/city` is the same closeness as Orbits, built as a neon city seen from above at an angle, in the manner of Tron (`src/demos/City.tsx`; the geometry in `src/demos/cityLayout.ts`, the camera in `src/demos/cityCamera.ts`). You are the tower in the middle, lit amber. Whoever is tagged `live-together` has a lit floor in it, in their own colour, with their name beside it, the closest at the top. Everyone else is a building on a dark grid round the tower, nearer it the closer they are: the distance is their closeness rank on exactly the scale Orbits uses (`rankFraction` in `src/demos/orbitsLayout.ts`, through `plotRadius`), so the two views agree on who is near, and avenues ring the closest 10, 25, 50 and so on as Orbits' guide rings do and in their colours, with buildings kept off them on the side their rank says (`bandOf`). Buildings stand in Orbits' order, A to Z clockwise, from a gap at the upper left where the avenues are labelled, pushed apart only as far as it takes not to overlap, each within its rank's band (`placeInBands`). People with no closeness yet are low slabs in the outskirts beyond the last avenue.

What else a building says: its height is the closeness score itself, on a log scale from the lowest slab to the tallest for the top score on the map (`buildingHeight`), so the skyline shows how strongly each friendship scores and not only its rank; its footprint is how much you have talked (`footprint`); and both taper away with rank past the closest few hundred (`taper`): a building's size is `TAPER_DOT + (1 − TAPER_DOT) / (1 + (rank / TAPER_KNEE)^TAPER_STEEP)` of what its score and messages alone would give, with the knee at rank 500, a power of 5 and a dot a tenth of full size, so the closest 250 are full size (97% at 250), rank 500 is just over half (55%), rank 1,000 a dot (13%), and nothing steps anywhere; it scales footprint and height alike, so volume goes as its cube. The Legend draws the curve. Its neon edges and faces are the person's identicon colour, the hue they picked included, the closest ten brightest and the rest fainter tier by tier exactly as Orbits draws them (`personOpacity`); a beacon pulses on the roof of everyone you should reach out to, in their colour (a floor of the tower pulses whole); and light runs down the road to the tower from everyone you have talked with in the last 7 days, most recent first, at most 80 roads.

Names are painted on the roofs: each name runs across the screen on its building's roof, squashed with the tilt as the roof is, as large as the roof carries it (`roofType`: a long name fits best across a diamond-on roof, a short one on a square-on roof) up to 18 px tall, and only once it would stand at least 8 px tall on the screen, so zooming in (or tilting toward straight down) names more and more of the city and far, tapered buildings carry no name until you are close. Whoever is picked out (hovered, or their panel open) is always readable: their name is bold on their roof when it stands 11 px tall there, and otherwise flat over the roof at full size. On a phone, as on Orbits, nobody is named but whoever is picked out. No name's key, `h`, takes them all off. Hovering a building (or a floor) shows the hover card, clicking opens the person panel, and whoever's panel is open stays picked out, with lines on the ground to the people they share group chats with and everyone else dimmed, as on Orbits. It zooms and pans as Orbits does (`src/demos/orbitsZoom.ts`: the wheel or a pinch, a drag, the + − 0 keys, the find box bringing someone into view), and the Query bar, Hide, Names (the names on the roofs are the choice's: first names, First L., in full, none, or only those picked) and the settings all apply as on Orbits, and it is in the demo at `/demo/city`; Methodology stays Orbits' own.

The camera turns and tilts as Google Maps' does (`src/demos/cityCamera.ts`): a drag with the middle or right mouse button, or with Ctrl held, turns the city round the tower as it goes sideways (the near side follows the pointer) and tilts it as it goes up (further over) or down (toward straight down, where the city is a map with no sides showing and every name flat), between straight down and 70°; on a touch screen two fingers twisted turn it and two fingers dragged up or down tilt it, while a pinch still zooms, each past a small deadzone so one gesture does not do the others. It first appears north up and tilted 60°, so a circle on the ground is twice as wide as tall, and a compass in the bottom-left corner shows the bearing and the lean and glides the camera back when pressed; `0` resets the zoom alone. Buildings are drawn from the back of the city to the front as the camera sees it, the tower among them, so a nearer building covers one behind it at every bearing, and roofs and names follow.

It is drawn on three canvases rather than SVG, with no new dependencies: hundreds of buildings each have three faces, glowing edges and a name, and the light and the beacons move every frame, which as SVG would be thousands of nodes restyled a frame, too much for a phone, while the Canvas 2D API redraws the whole city in a few milliseconds, turning included. The ground (grid, avenues, the lines to a picked-out person's friends, the moving light) is the bottom canvas, redrawn every frame while anything moves; the buildings and names the middle one, redrawn only when the layout, the zoom, the camera or who is picked out changes; the beacons the top one. Canvases are drawn at no more than 2× the screen's pixels, and with `prefers-reduced-motion` nothing moves. The city rises out of the ground when it first appears.

## Spider web

The same map as a spider's web, at `/spider` (Spider web, its own entry in the view switcher, after Circles; again at `/demo/spider` over the made-up crowd), the view the app opens on: the bare address `/`, `/demo` and any address that names no view lead to it (`DEFAULT_VIEW` in `src/views.ts`), built on Orbits' own layout and engine rather than a copy: `OrbitMap` with the web look (`src/demos/Orbits.tsx`), so closeness, the rings, the rules, hover, the panel, zoom, the find box, the Query bar, Methodology, Names and the usage record all apply as on Orbits, and it runs on whatever data the app has. What changes is what is drawn behind the people, and how they move, and that its sun is always the sun: the Spider web never asks for your photo nor offers its "+" and "×" (`photoShown` in `Orbits.tsx`). A canvas under the SVG (`src/demos/WebLayer.tsx`, the drawing in `src/demos/webLook.ts` with `webDraw.ts`) draws a holographic wireframe on a deep ground with a fine grid and scanlines: a spider's web (`src/demos/webGeometry.ts`), a clean orb web whose straight spokes run from the hub out past every edge of the screen, evenly round it with none straight up, and whose rings of cross threads from spoke to spoke are the guide rings (see **Strand curve** and **Spokes** below), the whole fading toward the edges so the people's part stays the clearest. The web is drawn beneath everyone: a hole of the ground's colour is cut under every marker, so the strands end at each icon's rim and never show through it (Orbits draws the fainter tiers' icons translucent), and the SVG above keeps everyone's identicon, trail and link exactly as Orbits draws them, recoloured, the names in monospace (the colour's theme, `src/demos/webTheme.ts`). When the Spider web opens it boots: a flicker, then a scan line from the top of the stage to the bottom that materialises the web as it passes, each person condensing in place as it reaches them, and locks each of the hundred closest in with a bracket, a flash that is gone again before the boot ends, never on a timer; after it no bracket stays on anyone, and the only one is the hover's (below). The boot plays again, the web and everyone vanishing and coming back with the scan in their new places, whenever you re-query: a new query in the Query bar (or an example in the demo), × back to everyone, or a change of the Table's Hide, even when it shows the same people (`requery` in `src/demos/queryBar.ts`, counted by `nextShown`, tested). Nothing else replays it: not the query run again as messages arrive or people change, nor hovering, opening a panel, the settings, zooming or resizing (`src/demos/webChoreography.ts` times the boot; `useTween`'s replay in `orbitsSky.ts` brings everyone in again, `tweenOf`, tested). Every other change of place (new messages, a tag) moves people as on Orbits, all at once over 0.7 seconds. Hovering someone while nobody's panel is open fires a beam at them from the spider's eyes (the middle of their cluster on the visor, turning with the head): it runs out, locks with a flash, and holds, energised, with light running down it, until they are looked away from, when it breaks up. The square reticle (3 pixels past their dot on each side, so its corners keep off the names) goes with the hover rather than the beam: it snaps onto whoever is hovered at once, whether or not someone's panel is open, and springs off as the pointer leaves them, both in a little over a tenth of a second so sweeping across many people stays readable (`MARK_TIMING` in `src/demos/webHold.ts`); with nobody held it flashes as the beam to them locks. Opening someone's panel takes them up instead (`src/demos/webHold.ts`, tested): a line is laid out to them from the mouth with a little slack, draws taut, and snaps with a flash and a twang into an energised beam, which holds them until the panel closes and then reels back into the mouth (a beam from the eyes already on them, because they were hovered as they were clicked, stays until the hold has them, then breaks up: on whoever is held the hold wins). A click or tap on empty map lets them go, closing the panel as its × does: on no one's dot (anyone's, in reach or not), name or ring label, and never a drag that pans (`emptyAt` in `src/demos/Orbits.tsx`, the view's `close`); the other views leave the panel open on such a click, closing it only with × or Escape; the held person gets no reticle, not even while hovered, so nothing more covers the names round them; while someone is held the spider looks only at them, and only they and the people they share group chats with are named, hovered or clicked (everyone else stays on the map, unnamed and out of reach of the pointer, until they are let go; their names stay where they were), so pointing at one of those shows their hover card and the reticle but fires no beam and turns nothing: while someone is held the hold is the only line. The beam, the hover's reticle and the hold, the hold over the other two, are drawn over everything, other people's icons included, on a second canvas above the SVG; the held person's links to their group chats' people flow outward too; and the hover card has one place for each person, whatever the pointer does on them: beside their dot, straight out from the spider, its nearest edge or corner 20 pixels from the dot's edge, so it covers neither the dot nor the beam; where that would leave the window it turns round the dot to the nearest place inside it that still keeps off both, and in a corner, where the beam comes in across the only room, it goes further out (`tipPlace` in `view.tsx`, tested). A finger held on someone shows the card above it, as on Orbits. The spider at the hub is an armoured machine in a real spider's shape, drawn from a rig of parts with lives of their own (`src/demos/webRig.ts` the rig, `webSpider.ts` the drawing, `webEyes.ts` the eyes): a small body on long legs. Each time the Spider web loads it starts facing a direction at random, already settled on its legs before the boot reveal, and under reduced motion stays still facing it; `?spider=<degrees>` in the address (clockwise from up, so `?spider=90` faces right) fixes that first direction instead, for tests and captures (`startHeading` in `src/demos/webLook.ts`). It is drawn as a thing standing on the web, lit from the top left of the screen: every plate is dark chitin with a lit face toward the light, a specular line along its lit edge, a rim of the web's own glow along its far edge and an emissive seam of the Spider web's accent down its middle, like a circuit trace, and every joint is a dark disc in a glowing ring; the legs are solved in three dimensions, so each joint keeps its height above the web, a steep segment is drawn darker than a flat one, and the whole spider casts a shadow on the web, each joint's shadow lying down and to the right of it by its height, so the knees' shadows stand far from the knees and a lifted foot's shadow comes away from it. The body: the abdomen, an egg hung from the pedicel at its own heading, its plates overlapping backward, a glowing seam with branching traces, a gloss on its shoulder and spinnerets at its tip, breathing slowly; the thorax, a shield over the hips with the fovea and its radial striae engraved; the neck, a glowing ball joint under two collar plates that turn with the head, with a cable each side; and the head, a faceted helmet with a glass visor across its front holding the eight eyes (the two big anterior median eyes in the middle, the lateral pair beside them, four smaller ones in the row behind), the chelicerae hanging under the face with their fangs curved in, and the pedipalps either side, waving slowly. The head looks toward whoever is looked at (whoever is held, else whoever is hovered) almost at once, within the range the neck can turn (the cursor itself means nothing until it is on someone); someone hovered within 45° either side of where the body faces (a cone of 90° in front, `CONE`) it looks at with its head alone, the body still, and someone hovered beyond that cone, or anyone selected, the head looks at first, then the chassis sets off a moment later and follows, slower, until it faces them, and the abdomen trails the chassis on a spring; it holds where it last turned. Each of its eight legs is a real spider's, six segments from the body out (the coxa, the femur, a spindle, up to the knee, the patella, the tibia and the metatarsus, with spines on their outer edge, and the tarsus, a needle to the foot, with its claws), and keeps a foothold on the web (a grip light marks each): where it bends is solved from its hip and its foothold, two bones twice (the femur against the shin, then the tibia against the metatarsus and tarsus) in a plane that leans back from the vertical, more as the leg folds, so from above the femurs fan out round the body like a star, the knees well outside it, and each leg arches from its knee down to its foot as a spider's does, and turns because its foot stays put, never as a rotated picture; the stance is a star, as a spider stands seen from above, the feet fanned round the body, each about as far from it, the front pair reaching forward, the second forward of sideways, the third just behind sideways and the rear pair back, and each leg stands and bends a little unlike its opposite and its neighbours (`QUIRKS`, chosen once by hand), so the pose is never mirror-perfect and always the same. As the chassis turns, no faster than its legs can carry it round (`CHASSIS_RATE`), a leg whose foothold has drifted from where it will rest once the turn is done (planned from where the body is going, so the legs set off with the turn rather than after it) steps toward it, lifting off the web, swinging a stride at most round the hub, so its foot keeps the stance's width through the turn instead of cutting across toward the body, a little outward at the middle, and planting with a soft settle, a leg already folded in stepping before any other, steps starting a few dozen milliseconds apart with several legs in the air at once but never a leg beside or across from one already in the air, the planted legs flexing as the body turns over them; the body leans a little away from whichever legs are in the air. At rest, now and then, never on a loop (a wait of two to nearly seven seconds drawn at random between), it does something of its own (`startIdle` in `webRig.ts`): a leg shifts its weight; a leg lifts high, plucks the web beside its foothold and steps back; a front leg folds up beside the face and back, the palps curling in, a groom; the head looks round, aside and back, slower than a look at someone; the body settles into a crouch, its hips nearer the web and its shadow drawing in, and rises; or the abdomen flexes and wags on its spring. Nothing of it while someone is looked at. Its eyes have a life of their own: blinks, double blinks, glances aside and slow scans at random moments, never on a loop, following whoever is hovered. It grows gently with the zoom, as its square root (`spiderGrowth` in `webLook.ts`; about three and a half times its size at the zoom's limit), slower than the people spread out, so it can be looked at closely and never reaches them; fine detail (the spines, the claws, the lit faces of the thinner segments) is drawn only once it would be more than a pixel, so it costs the same at the hub's size. Under reduced motion the web is drawn complete, people appear in place, every part of the spider goes straight to where it is headed, its eyes are still, nothing breathes or waves and nothing moves.

## The Query bar

Under the header of Orbits and the Table, together at the right: one box, with the placeholder "Query in natural language", and on the Table **Hide**. The map views have no Hide of their own: they follow the Table's, which is where people are sifted (archived, commercial and the like), so on the map the box is the whole query (`src/demos/QueryBar.tsx`, with what it holds and how it changes in `src/demos/queryBar.ts`; Hide in `src/demos/HidePanel.tsx` and `src/demos/hideChoices.ts`). There is no box to type a query in: you say who to show, and see who that is. How to ask, in short, is under "Asking who to show" in Methodology on the map.

- **The box** is who you want to see, in words: dictate into it (OpenSuperWhisper types into whichever box has focus) or type. Enter, or the go button inside the box at its right edge (a tick, pressable only with words in the box), has Codex write a SQL query from the words (Generate, below) and runs it straight away: the view shows the people it matches, and the line under the box says how many it shows after Hide, as Hide is when you press Enter, and anything the model could not put in the query ("12 people match. Not in the query: who like pizza (nothing in the archive says what people like)."), so it agrees with the header. Changing Hide afterwards leaves the line as it was; the header counts as Hide changes. When no query can be written, or it does not run, or the map refuses it as too many, nothing changes and the line says why. The query itself is never shown. Each press writes a new query from all the words in the box, so to narrow it, say the whole thing again ("climbers on Telegram", not "only the ones on Telegram"). Shift and Enter makes a new line; Esc puts back the words of what is showing. × (while the box holds words, or a query is on) shows everyone again ("Cleared: everyone shows."); there is no Undo. While a query is on, the box is edged in your colour. The box is at least 200 px wide and grows wider with its longest line into the bar's free width, and grows down over the page to show all it holds while you type in it; the line under it takes the width it needs.
- **The query that ran** decides who the view shows, and then Hide leaves some out: the header counts "18 of 80 people", Hide counts over the query's matches, and ranks and rings run over whoever is left. It runs in the server (`GET /api/archive/query`, `server/archive/sqlQuery.ts` for SQL, `query.ts` for the query language) over everyone, archived people too (Orbits and the Table each keep their own), and runs again whenever people or messages change; on both views it is also counted under Hide (`hide=` with each ticked tag, which the route answers with `shown`, how many of the matches have none of them), and again whenever Hide changes. **Orbits draws at most 1,000 people** (`SHOWN_LIMIT` in `src/archive.ts`): a query that would show more after Hide, such as everyone archived (archive unticked with no query: tens of thousands of people, which froze the page), is refused before any of it loads (`answered` in `src/demos/queryBar.ts`), and the map keeps what it showed. The line under the box says why ("1,234 people match; ask for fewer or hide more in the Table: the limit is 1,000."), and the box turns red, as they do for a query that does not run. The red clears once the words or Hide change, or a query runs. Changing Hide counts the query again under it: the refused query, if one is waiting (so ticking more in Hide can bring it under the limit and run it), else the one that ran. Opened with a refused query or Hide remembered, the map draws nobody and says why; × or the Table's Hide changes it. The Table has no limit: it draws only the rows in sight, so it lists everyone at once; a query that stops running (its tag deleted, say) shows nobody, and says why, until it is changed. No query is everyone. The query that ran, and the words it came from, are remembered per browser (`friend-map.query` and `friend-map.query.said`, `friend-map.table.query` and `friend-map.table.query.said`); a query an older page kept, in the query language and with no words, still runs, the box empty and edged, until × or a new request replaces it.
- **Hide** is the last gate after the query: a panel listing tags, each with how many of the query's matches have it and a tick; anyone with a ticked tag is left out, and "Select all" or "Unselect all" ticks or unticks every row. **archive** (everyone archived, by you or by the rules, see "Who is archived" under "The message archive" in the life archive's README) and **commercial** start ticked, and are listed even when nobody has them yet, so they take effect as soon as someone does; every other tag starts unticked. So "location:brisbane" shows the people in Brisbane but not the archived ones; untick archive and the query's archived matches come back, to look at who was archived again. On the map, archived people are loaded only once archive is unticked, and only those the query matches, so its row shows "…" rather than a count until then; and only when the query and Hide together show 1,000 people or fewer (see above): unticking archive with no query, which would show everyone archived, is refused, the map's box turning red, and the ticks stay as chosen so you can ask for fewer or tick more. The map lists the tags in use, most used first, and the Table every tag in the tag table, in its order; then both list either of the two not listed yet, in a fixed place, so ticking one never moves its row. The button, on the Table (and the Calendar's own), is an icon alone, an eye struck through, square and as tall as the box; how many are ticked is only in its tooltip. On the map views the find box, Methodology and the box share one height. On the map views the line under the box says how many are shown ("702 people") whenever everyone shows, and what the query matched while one is on. The ticks are remembered per browser (`friend-map.table.hide`), the map views reading the Table's; the maps' own ticks from before (`friend-map.hide`) are cleared, so they start from the Table's; ticks kept from when the rules' archive had a row of its own carry over (`oneArchive` in `src/demos/hideChoices.ts`): an old "system-archive" tick folds into archive, which stays unticked only if both were, and an old "spam" tick (spam was ticked by default then) is dropped. What the old Filter and Hide panels kept (`friend-map.tags`, `friend-map.table.tags`, `friend-map.tagPanels`) is cleared, so Hide starts from its two.

The query language, for the CLI's `--query` (and queries kept from before), in full at the top of `server/archive/query.ts`:

```
is-programmer                  everyone with the tag, written as tags are (lowercase, dashes for spaces)
is-*   *-weed                  * is any run of characters; tag:NAME, or "NAME", for a tag named like a word below
location:brisbane   where they are, as far as you last knew (see "People" in the life archive's README): location:new-york or location:"New York"; met: and lives: are gone, and say so
platform:telegram              an account on the platform
group:"Climbing crew"          in a group chat of that title, as its header lists them (members, and anyone who
                               wrote there); group:#75 one chat by its number (the backend's; the life archive's npm run archive -- groups)
should-reach-out               also untagged (none of your tags; archive and keep aside) and archived
messages>100                   also direct-messages and group-messages; < <= > >= =
last-spoke<2025-01             the last message's local day against a year, month or day: <2025-01 before
                               January 2025, >2025-01 after it, =2025-01 during it; never spoken to matches none
a AND NOT (b OR c)             NOT binds tightest, then AND, then OR; two terms need AND or OR between them
```

AND, OR, NOT, tags, fields and values may be written in any case. "archive" (or `tag:archive`) matches everyone archived, by you or the rules, as the `archived` flag does; "system-archive" is no longer a tag. A tag or value nobody could have (none in the tag table, or no one with it among everyone) is an error, never an empty map. What people do is a tag, such as is-programmer.

**Generate** writes SQL (see "SQL queries" in the life archive's README), so whatever the archive can answer becomes one complete query: "people on Discord I've sent more than 10 messages to" is `SELECT person_id FROM person_platform WHERE platform = 'discord' AND direct_from_me > 10`, counting only your own messages, on Discord alone. What the model is sent (`server/archive/queryWriter.ts`, `POST /api/archive/query/generate`): the five views with what each column means (catch-ups among them, with what "caught up with" means), the tables a query may need with their columns, the two functions, how to quote, and that Discord's data package holds only your own messages; every tag and each field's values as stored, with how many people, archived people too, have each, and how many are archived; today's date; and the words; never anyone's name, aliases, note or messages, and not the group chats' titles. It answers with the query and each condition the words ask for, saying whether the query has it; any it could not put in is the note under the box, so nothing is dropped quietly. Its query is run before it is sent back: one that does not run, or is not SQL, is sent back to the model once with the error, and if it still does not run, the error is what the line says. The route also takes a current query to change (`query`; one in the query language goes with a short account of what it means, to be rewritten as SQL), which the box never sends. Generate only writes a query; people are changed from a chat instead (see "Changing data from a chat" in the life archive's README). It asks through the server's AI service (`server/ai.ts`, see "The AI service" in the life archive's README).
